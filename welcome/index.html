01 / THE CHALLENGE

A CHANGING GROWING ENVIRONMENT

Farming is a
climate decision.

Farmers around the world navigate shifting temperatures, changing rainfall patterns, water shortages, extreme weather and declining soil health.

Those pressures make it difficult to choose rotations that protect fields, conserve water and support long-term resilience. A crop that improves soil in one place may use too much water, conflict with local markets or miss the growing window somewhere else.

02 / THE DECISION

CONTEXT CHANGES EVERYTHING

One crop plan
doesn’t fit every field.

A rotation is a sequence of trade-offs, not a universal recipe.

Water use, rooting depth, cover between seasons, nitrogen-fixing potential, pest cycles, erosion, forage value, equipment, planting dates and market access all matter. The same crop can be a good option in one farm and a poor fit in another.

04 / A TOOL, NOT A VERDICT

EXPLAINABLE DECISION SUPPORT

Make uncertainty
visible.

Soliq is designed to help farmers ask better questions — not to replace their judgement or an agronomist’s local expertise.

The prototype uses five nearby historical weather years to create conditional scenarios for the rest of 2026. Its ranges describe those scenarios, not calibrated probabilities or a guaranteed forecast. Missing field evidence and limitations remain visible so each comparison can be reviewed before action.

IMPORTANT MODEL BOUNDARYThe current water-balance model does not predict yield, profit, soil carbon, nitrogen credits, pest outcomes or crop health.

HOW THE PROTOTYPE WORKS

Evidence in.
Trade-offs out.

A transparent workflow lets a farmer inspect what went into a comparison, what the model calculated and what still needs local review.

01 / OBSERVE

Read the weather record.

The current prototype requests daily NASA POWER minimum and maximum temperature and corrected precipitation. It pairs available 2026 observations with complete historical years from 2016–2025.

OUTPUT / VERIFIED COVERAGE + SOURCE METADATA
02 / SET THE FIELD

Add local context.

Farm settings describe the point location, available soil-water capacity, starting storage, effective irrigation limit and the crop calendar being considered. These declared values stay visible in the exported evidence record.

INPUT / FARMER-DECLARED CONDITIONS
03 / SIMULATE

Compare three strategies.

The existing rotation is compared with two editable alternatives. A daily water-bucket calculation tracks crop-water demand, modeled storage and unmet demand under the same conditional weather scenarios.

COMPARISON / BASELINE + TWO ALTERNATIVES
04 / REVIEW

Explain what changes.

Results expose scenario ranges, water trade-offs, assumptions and source gaps. Language models can explain the numerical output, but the numerical engine — not the language model — supplies the calculations.

Different observations answer different questions. Soliq currently uses NASA POWER; the other sources below are potential complementary evidence layers for future development, not integrations claimed by this release.

01 / SOIL MOISTURE

SMAP

Surface soil moisture and freeze/thaw context can help assess dry-down, water availability and drought-related stress.

NASA MISSION ↗ 02 / PRECIPITATION

GPM / IMERG

Precipitation rate and accumulation can reveal rainfall timing, heavy events, seasonal reliability and potential erosion pressure.

Landsat · MODIS · VIIRS

Reflectance and vegetation indices can help track crop vigor, seasonal change, bare-ground windows and vegetation stress.

RESEARCH & VALIDATION

Beyond a ranking.
Show the uncertainty.

A useful recommendation should be explainable, traceable and willing to say when the evidence is not strong enough.

The research direction asks whether identifying the uncertain inputs that can change a rotation decision — and choosing the next useful measurement — can reduce decision risk more effectively than relying on one point estimate.

The current brief separates implemented calculations from proposed experiments. Independent field outcomes, multi-region tests, satellite-source integration, farmer and agronomist evaluation, and prospective validation are still needed.
