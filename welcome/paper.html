WORKING RESEARCH BRIEF / SOIL-IQ

When Does Better Evidence Change a Crop-Rotation Decision?

Mohamed Mostafa, Youssef Mohamed, Aya Abdelnabi, and Mohamed Abdelrahman

NASA Space Apps project team · 2026

RESEARCH STATUS / WORKING DRAFTThis document describes the current prototype and proposed evaluation. It is not peer reviewed, does not establish field performance, and is not a submitted journal manuscript.

Abstract

We propose a framework for identifying fragile crop-rotation choices under limited environmental and management information. The current prototype combines historical NASA POWER point weather, declared soil storage, crop coefficients, and explicit feasibility checks. Eligible crop alternatives are compared using paired water-deficit scenarios. Decision reversals and maximum finite-scenario regret expose sensitivity that a single ranking conceals. A future evidence-acquisition module will test whether targeted measurements reduce decision loss more efficiently than untargeted data collection. No field outcomes or superiority claims are established here.

Research question

Under a fixed evidence-collection budget, can identifying which uncertain inputs change a rotation decision reduce held-out decision loss and inappropriate recommendations compared with point-estimate ranking and uniform evidence collection?

Related work and contribution boundary

Geoinformatics, stochastic optimization, crop suitability dashboards, remote-sensing rotation matrices, and value-of-information methods already exist. Their combination is not by itself a novelty claim. The proposed contribution is a jointly evaluated workflow connecting evidence provenance and spatial support to ranking reversals, selective abstention, and the next useful measurement. Its novelty remains a hypothesis requiring a systematic review.

Implemented method

Six crop profiles use declared growth-stage durations and FAO-56 starting coefficients. A daily fixed-depth water bucket carries storage through the annual calendar. Four shared scenarios comprise reference inputs, 25% less rainfall (subject to the model's lower rainfall-scale bound), and ±15% crop coefficients within the comparison window. Local hard exclusions block simulation; missing checks and synthetic forcing prevent shortlisting.

For candidate c and scenario s, let L(c,s) be annual unmet modeled water demand in millimeters. Regret is R(c,s) = L(c,s) − min over eligible alternatives a of L(a,s). The displayed maximum is max over the four tested scenarios of R(c,s). A pair reverses when the sign of its loss difference changes across tested scenarios. Ties use a 10⁻⁶ mm numerical tolerance.

Limits and next work

The prototype does not model yield, profit, soil carbon, nitrogen credits, salinity, flooded systems, crop-specific frost tolerance, or multi-year soil processes. Historical weather is not a seasonal forecast. SMAP, IMERG, Landsat, and ECOSTRESS fusion, spatial-support auditing, measurement acquisition, and multi-region field validation remain unimplemented. The full earlier manuscript is not included in this release; this brief documents the current implementation honestly and is not an IEEE JSTARS submission.
